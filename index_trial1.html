Forward Kinematics

Forward kinematics maps a joint configuration to an end-effector pose.

\[ \mathbf{x} = f(\mathbf{q}) \] \[ \dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{J}\dot{\mathbf{q}}\\ \] \[ \dot{\mathbf{q}} = \mathbf{J}^{-1}\dot{\mathbf{x}}, \det(\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{q})) \neq 0 \]

From Joint Motion to Cartesian Motion

Small changes in joint configuration produce motion of the end-effector.

The differential relationship is described by the geometric Jacobian.

\[ \boldsymbol{\xi} = {\color{red}{J(\mathbf{q})}} \dot{\mathbf{q}} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{T}(\mathbf{q}) &= e^{[\mathcal{S}_1]q_1} e^{[\mathcal{S}_2]q_2} \cdots e^{[\mathcal{S}_n]q_n} \mathbf{M} \\[4pt] &= \prod_{i=1}^{n} e^{[\mathcal{S}_i]q_i} \mathbf{M} \end{aligned} \]
\[ J(\mathbf{q}) = \begin{bmatrix} \vert & \vert & & \vert \\ \mathcal{S}_1' & \mathcal{S}_2' & \cdots & \mathcal{S}_n' \\ \vert & \vert & & \vert \end{bmatrix} \]
